# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The public: anyone who finds cat.log, with no explanation from us. The typical session is a few friends in the same room, in the evening, in no hurry: two people passing one iPad back and forth, or a small group with a device each. Players may be new to the game, so every screen must explain itself at a glance. The job to be done is a relaxed, low-stakes game together: set up quickly, play a round, laugh a little, play another.

## Product Purpose

cat.log is a catalog of small, cozy, cat-themed games for playing together (the name is a pun on "catalog" and a `.log` file). The landing page presents the catalog; each game lives at its own route. Meowstermind is a two-player Mastermind with cats for code pins and paw prints for clues, played on one shared screen. Monopawly is a cat-town property game for 2 to 6 players in private online rooms, each on their own device. More will follow.

Success looks like: two strangers open a game and are playing within a minute, without reading instructions twice; they finish a match and start another; the site feels like a small, lovingly made thing rather than a product trying to hook them.

## Brand Personality

**Cozy, sly, gentle.**

Calm and unhurried at heart, with a quiet mischievous streak: sneaky cat energy, hidden codes, eyes lurking in the dark, a sleepy cat that "won't tell." Warm rather than loud; charming rather than cutesy. The voice is soft and a little playful ("Purrfect!", "zzz… the next one is still napping"), never shouty, salesy or cloying.

Reference: **Neko Atsume**. Gentle, low-pressure cat time; simple and sweet; delight that comes from small details rather than spectacle.

## Anti-references

- **Mobile-game casino**: flashing rewards, coins, streaks, ads, pushy "play again!" prompts, anything resembling a dark pattern.
- **Loud neon gamer**: RGB glows, aggressive angles, esports energy.
- **Childish or babyish**: primary colors, clip art, toddler-toy styling. Cute, but made for grown-ups.
- **Generic SaaS / AI slop**: gradient blobs, glassy cards, stock icons, templated hero-and-feature-grid landing pages.

## Design Principles

1. **Calm first, cute second.** No pressure, no timers nagging, no noise competing for attention. Delight should feel like a purr, not an alarm.
2. **The mischief is in the details.** Personality lives in small, discoverable touches (lurking eyes, sleepy cats, twitching ears, a burst of kittens on a win), never in loud decoration.
3. **Play comes before chrome.** During a game, the board and the players' next move outrank everything else on screen. Decoration must never obscure state or slow a turn.
4. **Charming for grown-ups.** Kawaii with restraint: considered, tasteful, never babyish.
5. **Fingers first.** Every interaction is designed for touch on an iPad before mouse: generous targets, forgiving drops, tap alternatives to drag.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Big, forgiving touch targets** are a hard requirement: comfortable for all ages on iPad, with enlarged drop zones for small targets and tap-to-place as an alternative to drag.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for decorative motion (already done for the kitten burst, blinking eyes and ear twitch).
- Keep text readable against the dark background and label interactive pieces for screen readers as a sensible baseline.
